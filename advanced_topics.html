Specialization Tracks Index

The following specialization tracks allow self-directed learners to deepen their understanding of political economy; learners are encouraged to select one or two tracks aligned with their specific intellectual or professional interests rather than attempting to study all of them.

  • Political Economy of Development - How political institutions, governance structures, and historical processes shape economic development outcomes across countries.
  • Political Economy of Finance & Financialization - How financial markets, systemic crises, and the expanding power of the financial sector restructure modern economies and state policy.
  • Environmental & Ecological Political Economy - How economic systems interact with ecological boundaries, climate policy instruments, and resource transitions.
  • Political Economy of Inequality & Redistribution - How income and wealth concentration emerge, and how democratic institutions and electoral systems mediate redistributive conflict.
  • Digital Political Economy & Platform Capitalism - How digital platforms, data extraction, and network monopolization reshape contemporary capitalist organization and political power.

Political Economy of Development

This track explores how political institutions, governance mechanisms, and historical processes determine divergent paths of economic growth and state capacity.

Institutional Foundations of Growth: Institutions as the Fundamental Cause of Long-Run Growth (Daron Acemoglu, Simon Johnson, & James Robinson / NBER) - The seminal working paper demonstrating empirically and theoretically that comparative economic development is driven primarily by institutional rules rather than geography or culture.

Political Economy of Underdevelopment: Political Economy and Economic Development (MIT OpenCourseWare / 14.75) - A comprehensive university course providing lecture notes and problem sets on collective action failures, bureaucratic corruption, political transitions, and the persistent effects of historical shocks.

Inclusive vs. Extractive Institutions: Why Nations Fail: The Origins of Power, Prosperity, and Poverty (Daron Acemoglu & James A. Robinson / Crown) - An accessible, case-study-rich synthesis examining how political institutions establish inclusive or extractive economic systems that either foster or block long-term innovation.

Escaping the Middle-Income Trap: World Development Report 2024: The Middle-Income Trap (World Bank) - A definitive policy report outlining the structural transitions from capital investment to technological infusion and domestic innovation required for emerging economies to sustain growth.

Political Economy of Finance & Financialization

This track examines the destabilizing dynamics of credit cycles, systemic financial crises, and the political dominance of the financial sector over modern macroeconomic policy.

Mechanics of Financial Crises: Financial Crises (MIT OpenCourseWare / 14.09) - An analytical lecture series and reading syllabus breaking down the amplification mechanisms of bank runs, collateral fire sales, leverage cycles, and regulatory design.

Endogenous Financial Fragility: The Financial Instability Hypothesis (Hyman P. Minsky / Levy Economics Institute) - The foundational post-Keynesian paper arguing that extended periods of macroeconomic stability endogenously induce speculative and Ponzi debt structures that culminate in systemic crises.

The Politics of Financialization: Capitalizing on Crisis: The Political Origins of the Rise of Finance (Greta R. Krippner / Harvard University Press) - A historical-sociological study showing how financialization developed out of state attempts to resolve domestic distribution disputes and stagflation during the 1970s and 1980s.

Advanced Macro-Financial Frictions: Economic Crises (MIT OpenCourseWare / 14.454) - Advanced graduate courseware featuring problem sets and lecture notes that rigorously formalize credit constraints, liquidity spirals, sovereign debt distress, and sudden stops.

Environmental & Ecological Political Economy

This track analyzes how economic systems interface with biophysical limits, evaluating climate mitigation tools, integrated assessment models, and energy transition politics.

Biophysical and Ecological Economics: Ecological Economics: Principles and Applications (Herman E. Daly & Joshua Farley / Island Press) - The definitive textbook grounding economic production within physical thermodynamic laws, shifting the analytical focus from endless throughput expansion to optimal scale and equitable distribution.

Climate Economics and Integrated Assessment: Economics and Policies of Climate Change (EDHEC Business School / Coursera) - A structured online course available in free audit mode covering the social discount rate, Nordhaus's DICE integrated assessment model, carbon pricing frameworks, and carbon trading mechanics.

Decarbonization and Energy Transitions: Energy Decisions, Markets, and Policies (MIT OpenCourseWare / 15.031J) - Complete open courseware including full video lectures that examine energy markets, regulatory path dependencies, and the political barriers to large-scale decarbonization.

The Social Cost of Carbon: Climate Change: The Ultimate Challenge for Economics (William D. Nordhaus / Nobel Prize Foundation) - The seminal Nobel Prize lecture presenting the orthodox integrated assessment paradigm for quantifying climate damage externalities and establishing global emissions pricing.

Political Economy of Inequality & Redistribution

This track investigates the drivers of wealth and income inequality alongside the electoral and institutional mechanisms that govern redistributive social policy.

Long-Run Capital and Wealth Dynamics: Capital in the Twenty-First Century (Thomas Piketty / Harvard University Press) - The benchmark empirical study compiling historical multi-century tax datasets to prove that when the rate of return on capital exceeds output growth, inherited wealth naturally concentrates.

Wealth Taxation and Fiscal Enforcement: Wealth Taxation: Lessons from History and Recent Developments (Emmanuel Saez & Gabriel Zucman / AEA Papers and Proceedings) - An open-access policy analysis examining historical European wealth tax designs, offshore avoidance avenues, and modern reporting mechanisms for effective wealth redistribution.

Electoral Rules and Coalition Formation: Electoral Institutions and the Politics of Coalitions: Why Some Democracies Redistribute More Than Others (Torben Iversen & David Soskice / American Political Science Review) - The foundational paper proving that proportional representation fosters center-left redistributive coalitions while majoritarian single-member districts systematically favor center-right governments; search title on Google Scholar for open-access working papers.

Resilience of Democratic Capitalism: Democracy and Prosperity: Reinventing Capitalism through a Turbulent Century (Torben Iversen & David Soskice / Princeton University Press) - A major treatise demonstrating how advanced democratic states and capitalist markets co-evolved, arguing that modern democratic governments actively re-engineer knowledge economies to preserve broad-based middle-class stability.

Digital Political Economy & Platform Capitalism

This track probes the political and structural dimensions of the digital economy, focusing on platform monopolization, behavioral surveillance, and data extractivism.

Architectural Dynamics of Platform Capitalism: Platform Capitalism (Nick Srnicek / Polity Press) - A foundational theoretical framework detailing how data collection serves as the central raw material of modern capitalism, examining multi-sided platform architectures and monopolistic lock-in effects.

Structural Dimensions of the Digital Economy: Digital Capitalism (Transnational Institute) - A free 6-week open-access course providing video masterclasses and reading packets on Big Tech monopolies, digital trade regimes, data sovereignty, and technological alternatives.

Surveillance Capitalism: The Age of Surveillance Capitalism: The Fight for a Human Future at the New Frontier of Power (Shoshana Zuboff / PublicAffairs) - The canonical treatise exposing how digital corporations convert private human experience into proprietary behavioral data used for behavioral modification and market control.

Data Colonialism: The Costs of Connection: How Data Is Colonizing Human Life and Appropriating It for Capitalism (Nick Couldry & Ulises A. Mejias / Stanford University Press) - An advanced critical analysis demonstrating how global data extraction models reproduce historical colonial practices by turning everyday human life into commodifiable inputs.
