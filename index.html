Hocbigg - Political Economy

Path to a free self-taught education in Political Economy.

Last updated on: 2026-10-08

Introduction

Political economy investigates how political authority, institutional rules, and economic processes shape the production and distribution of wealth. Where orthodox economics frequently treats markets as self-contained mechanisms and mainstream political science often studies state institutions in isolation from material conditions, political economy bridges the two. It examines who benefits from economic arrangements, how power structures markets, and how economic interests influence state action.

This curriculum is built for independent, self-directed learners coming to the subject without prior training in economics, political science, or advanced mathematics.

No formal prerequisites are assumed. The technical depth of your study is largely up to you: the microeconomics and macroeconomics modules offer both a problem-centered, institutional pathway (via CORE Econ) that requires only basic graphical literacy, and a mathematically formal university pathway (via MIT OpenCourseWare) for learners comfortable with single-variable calculus. Whichever track you choose, the primary objective throughout this core is analytical fluency—understanding the mechanisms that drive economic and political outcomes, rather than mastering pure academic econometrics or abstract theorem proving.

How to Move Through the Curriculum

The curriculum consists of six foundational subjects arranged in a deliberate learning progression:

  • Start with the foundations: Begin with Foundations of Political Economy. This grounds you in the competing theoretical traditions—principally liberal, Marxian, and institutionalist—that define how scholars interpret the relationship between states and markets.
  • Build your analytical toolkit: Move to Principles of Microeconomics and Principles of Macroeconomics. Microeconomics supplies the tools for analyzing individual incentives, firm behavior, market failures, and strategic interactions. Macroeconomics examines economy-wide aggregates, including inflation, employment, and fiscal and monetary policy. You can tackle these sequentially (microeconomics first is standard) or alongside each other.
  • Ground the theories in intellectual history: Proceed to History of Economic Thought & Classical Political Economy. Here you will engage with foundational texts by Adam Smith, Karl Marx, and John Maynard Keynes. Studying these thinkers directly clarifies that economic doctrines are historical products developed to explain specific social crises, rather than timeless laws of nature.
  • Apply the toolkit to domestic and global systems: Conclude with Comparative Political Economy and International Political Economy, which can be studied in either order once your micro, macro, and theoretical foundations are in place. Comparative Political Economy examines how national institutions, labor regimes, and welfare states vary across modern democracies. International Political Economy scales this inquiry to the international arena, exploring cross-border trade, monetary regimes, multinational production, and global governance.

Scope

This page is strictly conservative in scope: it covers only the core knowledge and analytical methods common to all major traditions of political economy. It does not attempt to cover specialized subfields, regional area studies, or niche policy debates.

Once you have completed this foundational core, you can pursue specialized tracks in Advanced Topics.

Curriculum

Foundations of Political Economy

This subject introduces the core paradigms structuring the discipline, analyzing how political power, institutions, and economic processes govern the distribution of wealth and resources.

Capitalism: Success, Crisis, and Reform (Yale Open Courses / Douglas Rae) - A comprehensive video lecture series that introduces the historical evolution and institutional tensions of modern capitalism; use this as your visual lecture foundation alongside the core textbooks.

Understanding Capitalism: Competition, Command, and Change (Oxford University Press / Bowles, Roosevelt, Edwards, Larudee) - The primary foundational textbook, offering an integrated overview of political economy through neoclassical, Marxian, and institutionalist lenses.

Analyzing Politics: Rationality, Behavior, and Institutions (W. W. Norton / Kenneth A. Shepsle) - A complementary textbook focusing on positive political economy and rational choice; read this alongside Bowles et al. to master formal models of collective action, voting rules, and institutional design.

Principles of Microeconomics

This subject covers individual and firm decision-making, market mechanisms, and strategic interactions, providing the foundational micro-level toolkit used in political economy.

Principles of Microeconomics (MIT OpenCourseWare / 14.01) - A mathematically rigorous, calculus-based video lecture course with problem sets and exams; choose this as your primary microeconomics option if you prefer standard neoclassical theory and formal problem-solving.

The Economy 2.0: Microeconomics (CORE Econ) - An alternative primary microeconomics track that uses an open-access, problem-centered format to teach economic principles through institutions, power, and inequality rather than abstract equilibrium math.

Microeconomics (Khan Academy) - A supplementary video and exercise library to consult selectively for quick, intuitive explanations of specific concepts that prove challenging in MIT or CORE Econ.

Principles of Macroeconomics

This subject examines economy-wide aggregates including output, employment, inflation, and fiscal and monetary policy through an analytical lens essential for evaluating state policy choices.

Principles of Macroeconomics (MIT OpenCourseWare / 14.02) - A complete university lecture course covering fluctuations, monetary and fiscal policy, and growth models; serves as your primary macroeconomics option if you want orthodox macroeconomic mechanics with assignments and exams.

The Economy 2.0: Macroeconomics (CORE Econ) - An alternative primary macroeconomics curriculum focused on real-world crises, unemployment, and institutional frictions; choose this if you prefer a contextualized, empirical approach to macro theory.

Macroeconomics (Khan Academy) - An auxiliary resource designed to be used alongside your primary coursework for straightforward visual walk-throughs of foundational macroeconomic diagrams like IS-LM and the Phillips curve.

History of Economic Thought & Classical Political Economy

This subject traces the evolution of economic and political thought through close study of foundational thinkers whose paradigms defined the discipline.

Great Economists: Classical Economics and Its Forerunners (MRUniversity / Tyler Cowen & Alex Tabarrok) - A structured series of over 100 short video lectures serving as an accessible visual entry point to classical economics from pre-Smithian thinkers through modern debates.

The Worldly Philosophers: The Lives, Times, and Ideas of the Great Economic Thinkers (Simon & Schuster / Robert Heilbroner) - An alternative or companion introductory narrative to the MRUniversity course, tracing the lives and central concepts of foundational thinkers in an engaging biographical format.

The Wealth of Nations (Project Gutenberg / Adam Smith) - The seminal primary text of classical liberalism; read Books I and IV in sequence after completing the MRUniversity or Heilbroner overview.

Capital: A Critique of Political Economy, Volume I (Marxists Internet Archive / Karl Marx) - The canonical critique of capitalist production and surplus extraction; read the core selections (Parts I, III, and VIII) in conjunction with David Harvey's companion lectures below.

Reading Marx's Capital with David Harvey (davidharvey.org) - A dedicated chapter-by-chapter video lecture companion designed specifically to be watched in tandem with your reading of Marx's Capital Volume I.

The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money (Marxists Internet Archive / John Maynard Keynes) - The foundational text of modern macroeconomic political economy; read Books I through III following your classical and Marxist readings to understand the theoretical shift toward aggregate demand.

Comparative Political Economy

This subject analyzes how national institutional architectures, labor systems, corporate governance, and welfare states vary across modern capitalist societies.

Comparative Political Economy: States, Markets and Global Capitalism (Bloomsbury / Ben Clift) - The primary introductory textbook for this subject, surveying comparative institutional frameworks, welfare states, and national growth models in clear, accessible prose.

Varieties of Capitalism: The Institutional Foundations of Comparative Advantage (Oxford University Press / Peter Hall & David Soskice, eds.) - The seminal academic volume establishing the Varieties of Capitalism framework; read the introductory chapter alongside Clift to examine the distinction between Liberal and Coordinated Market Economies.

International Political Economy

This subject explores how political power and economic systems intersect across national borders, focusing on global trade, international finance, development, and transnational governance.

Political Economy of Institutions and Development (Coursera / Leiden University) - A structured 10-module foundational video course exploring governance, globalization, trust, and global development; use this as your primary lecture-based introduction.

Global Political Economy: Evolution and Dynamics (Bloomsbury / Robert O'Brien & Marc Williams) - The primary global textbook for the subfield, pairing with the Leiden course to provide exhaustive, historically rich coverage of global trade, production, finance, and international organizations.

International Political Economy (Routledge / Thomas Oatley) - An alternative or complementary textbook focusing heavily on the analytical, interest-based American approach to international trade and monetary relations.
